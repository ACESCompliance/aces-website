What it looks like

What does a shift handoff look like before and after the Sprint?

This is the first task we take on at most plants. Same supervisor, same shift, same report your plant already uses. The sample below is illustrative; the format comes from your plant, not ours.

Before

End of second shift, 2:50pm

35–45 minutes

The lead walks off the floor to a desk, opens last shift's report, deletes it line by line, and retypes the day from memory and a notebook. The downtime on Line 3 gets one line because he's tired. Third shift reads it at 3:05, or doesn't.

What he's working from
L3 down 11:40–12:25 bearing, maint called L1 ran 4,180 / plan 4,400 short staffed 2 new temp on L2 pack, needs sign-off near miss forklift dock door 2 — talked to J. 3rd shift: watch L3, PM tonight?
After

End of second shift, 2:55pm

5 minutes

The lead talks the same notes into his phone as a voice note, in the tool the plant already pays for. The report comes back in the plant's handoff format. He reads it, corrects the Line 1 count, and sends. Third shift gets the open items at the top.

What comes back
Shift Handoff — 2nd shift — Lines 1–3 OPEN ITEMS (3) 1. Line 3 bearing — maintenance called 12:25; confirm PM tonight. 2. Line 2 pack — new temp needs sign-off before running solo. 3. Dock door 2 — forklift near miss; coaching done, review traffic pattern. Production L1 4,180 / 4,400 plan (short 2 heads). L3 down 11:40–12:25. Safety 1 near miss (dock 2), 0 injuries. For 3rd shift Watch L3 after restart.
Illustrative sample. Your plant's headings, your plant's lines.

Multiply by two shifts a day, five days a week, and one lead gets back roughly 6 hours a week on this task alone. The Sprint does this for two to four tasks, with every lead on the plant, and counts it.

The Sprint, day by day

What happens in the 30-day AI Adoption Sprint?

One plant. Up to 25 supervisors and leads. Tools you already license (Microsoft 365 Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude or similar). Nothing to install, nothing to build.

Day 0

Twenty-minute call

You name the task that eats the most supervisor time. I tell you whether it's a good first target and what the Sprint would take at your plant.

Day 1

Half-day leadership working session

Plant manager, ops, HR/training, EHS. We pick the two to four tasks, agree what "working" means in hours, and write the rules: which data stays out, which tool is approved, what AI is never allowed to touch.

Days 2–5

Task packs built on your forms

For each task I build the prompt, the template in your format, a one-page card for the clipboard, and a short training clip, using your real handoff sheet, your incident form, your training matrix.

Day 6

Train-the-trainer with your lead supervisors

Hands-on, on their own tasks from last week. They run the method until it's boring, then they learn to teach it in 15 minutes at a shift huddle. It doesn't leave when I do.

Days 7–30

Rollout and scorecard

Lead supervisors roll it to their shifts. I check in weekly, fix what's stuck, and keep the usage scorecard: who's using it, on which tasks, minutes before and after.

Day 30

Written read-out

Hours returned per task, adoption by shift, what stuck, what didn't, and what to take on next. A number, not a feeling.

Questions

Straight answers.

What is an AI Adoption Sprint?

A 30-day engagement for one manufacturing plant. Two to four supervisor admin tasks (shift handoffs, incident write-ups, training records, work orders) move onto AI tools the plant already owns, lead supervisors are trained to teach the new method to their shifts, and a usage scorecard measures how many hours came back.

Do we have to buy new software?

No. The Sprint runs on tools most plants already license: Microsoft 365 Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude or similar. If you have none of them, we pick one together on the first call. The cost of the tool is a rounding error next to supervisor hours.

Is this about replacing supervisors?

No, and we say that out loud on the floor on day one. The work targets the paperwork around the job, not the job. Supervisors who get an hour back spend it on the line with their people. That's where incidents get prevented and output gets made.

What about confidential production or safety data?

The leadership session sets the rules before anyone types anything: which data stays out, which tools are approved, and how records are kept. Enterprise versions of these tools don't train on your data. We work inside whatever your IT policy allows.

How is this different from AI training?

AI training teaches people about a tool and leaves. The Sprint starts from named tasks on your own forms, trains your lead supervisors to teach their shifts, and tracks usage for 30 days so you have a number, not a feeling.

Where do you work?

Based in Pennsylvania and Florida. On site anywhere in the US. The leadership session and train-the-trainer also run well remotely for multi-site operations.
